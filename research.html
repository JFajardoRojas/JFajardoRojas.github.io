Research

Finding better porous materials, faster

Porous materials such as metal–organic frameworks can be built in millions of combinations, far more than can be simulated or made. My work turns that search into a loop: simulate a few, learn from them, choose the next ones wisely, and keep only materials that can exist.

The same loop, built for gas adsorption, is the starting point for catalysis.

The discovery loop
A closed loop for discovering adsorbents. Simulate, represent, learn, select, validate.
01

Representations that carry physics

Adsorption depends on how a guest molecule interacts with the framework, not only on pore geometry. I build representations from that physics, such as two-dimensional histograms of interaction parameters, which are simple to compute and work across materials and gases.

  • Two-dimensional interaction parameter histograms as a simple and versatile nanoporous material representation for machine learning prediction of adsorption propertiesMol. Syst. Des. Eng., 2026
  • Machine learning to design metal–organic frameworks: progress and challenges from a data efficiency perspectiveMater. Horiz., 2026
02

Learning from less data

Screening thousands of materials across many gases and conditions is expensive, even with simulation. Active and transfer learning decide which simulations to run and reuse what models already know, moving toward a universal adsorption model.

  • Active learning of alchemical adsorption simulations: towards a universal adsorption modelChem. Sci., 2024
  • MOFs to enhance green NH₃ synthesis in plasma reactors: hierarchical computational screening enhanced by iterative machine learningACS Appl. Mater. Interfaces, 2024
03

From predicted to makeable

A great adsorbent only matters if it can be made and stays intact. Free-energy calculations, accelerated with machine learning, tell us which frameworks are stable; expert-guided language models read synthesis procedures from the literature to connect predictions with the lab.

  • Highly accurate and fast prediction of MOF free energy via machine learningJ. Am. Chem. Soc., 2025
  • Data-driven insights on the impact of functionalization on metal–organic framework free energiesChem. Mater., 2025
  • Interactions of common synthesis solvents with MOFs studied via free energies of solvationChem. Mater., 2026
  • Expert-guided LLM approach for sequence-aware extraction of MOF synthesisChemRxiv, 2025
NEXT

Toward catalysis

Adsorption is the first step of every catalytic cycle, and the confinement that makes a pore selective also shapes reactivity. The next step is to extend the discovery loop to catalytic materials: descriptors of active sites in their pore environment, machine-learned potentials for binding, and stability under reaction conditions.
