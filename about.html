I am a computational materials scientist working at the intersection of molecular simulation and machine learning. My goal is to shorten the path from a promising idea to a useful porous material, first for gas adsorption and separation, and next for catalysis.

I am a {{ site.position | downcase }} in the group of Eric Toberer at the {{ site.institution }}, within the NSF Institute for Data-Driven Dynamical Design (ID4). I earned my PhD in Materials Science at Mines in 2025, advised by Diego Gómez-Gualdrón. My thesis, Data-Driven Development of Metal–Organic Frameworks: Synthesizability Insights and Thermodynamic Stability Predictions, combined free-energy calculations and machine learning to ask which predicted frameworks can actually exist.

Before that, I was an experimentalist. At Universidad de los Andes in Colombia I earned a PhD in Engineering with Diego Pradilla and Oscar Alvarez, studying how asphaltenes and other surface-active molecules behave at fluid–fluid interfaces. I started as a chemical engineer at Universidad Industrial de Santander, where I also earned an MSc in Hydrocarbon Engineering and worked on reservoir simulation for enhanced oil recovery. That path, from the lab bench to the computer, shapes how I build models: they should answer questions experimentalists actually have.
